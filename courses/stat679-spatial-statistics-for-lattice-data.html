STAT 679: Spatial Statistics for Lattice Data

Spring 2026 | University of Wisconsin-Madison | Graduate (MS)

Overview

This course will cover spatial statistical methods for lattice data, with emphasis on modeling spatial dependence, inference, computation, and applications to structured spatial and areal data.

Course Objective

The course is directed towards graduate students who are interested in analyzing spatial data, including students from the environmental and ecological sciences, urban and regional planning, soil sciences, plant and animal sciences, and statistics. Similar to STAT 679-I Foundations and STAT 679-II Regression & Big Data, this course will focus mostly on methods, although an understanding of concepts and of the underlying ideas will be stressed, as opposed to simply providing a cookbook of statistical formulas.

Requisites

To take the course, students must have STAT 679-I Foundations and STAT 679-II Regression & Big Data, or consent of instructor.

Learning Outcomes

After completing STAT 679, a student should be able to:

  • Demonstrate foundational concepts in spatial statistics
    • Classify spatial data into geostatistical, areal, and point pattern data.
    • Summarize spatial data by appropriate graphs and statistics.
    • Recognize spatial data as realizations of a random process.
  • Understand foundational spatial regression concepts
    • Apply rudimentary spatial regression models.
    • Construct hypothesis tests for regression coefficients.
    • Evaluate model assumptions.
    • Select models based on appropriate criteria.
  • Perform spatial regression analysis and interpret results for spatial data
    • Use a statistical software package to aid in the analysis of spatial data.
    • Draw conclusions based on hypothesis tests and model selection.
    • Communicate the results in the context of the study questions.
