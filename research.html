Projects

Current Projects

Wildlife Disease Modeling

Developing statistical and machine learning methods for ecological data, wildlife contact processes, and disease dynamics. This project focuses on connecting spatial, temporal, and network information to better understand disease risk in wildlife systems.

Funding:NIFA and USGS
Affiliation:Postdoctoral Research Associate at UW-Madison Statistics
Collaborators:Jun Zhu and Dan Walsh
Status:Current
Keywords:Wildlife disease, ecological data science, spatial statistics

Past Projects

Seismic Event Discrimination

Building deep learning models to classify earthquakes and explosions using multi-component waveform data. The work emphasizes both out-of-distribution generalization and interpretability so model decisions can be evaluated in scientific context.

Funding:LANL Research Fund
Affiliation:Summer Intern at Los Alamos National Laboratory in 2024, with continued collaboration through doctoral research at Washington State University
Collaborators:Ting Chen and Carene Larmat
Status:Completed
Keywords:Scientific AI, seismic waveforms, interpretability, generalization

Change Point Detection in Networks

Studying spectral and statistical methods for identifying temporal changes in evolving networks. The goal is to detect meaningful shifts in latent structure, dimensionality, or connectivity patterns in complex network data.

Funding:To be updated
Affiliation:Washington State University / Vassar College collaboration
Status:Completed
Keywords:Change point detection, random dot product graphs, dynamic networks

Network Aggregation and Fairness

Modeling aggregation of school-level friendship networks while accounting for language, identity, and representation risk. This work examines how network summaries and modeling choices can affect downstream interpretation.

Funding:To be updated
Affiliation:Washington State University
Status:Completed
Keywords:Network aggregation, fairness, social networks

Graph Representation Learning

Investigating embedding and representation learning methods for network data, including stochastic block models and random dot product graphs. The project connects graph machine learning with statistical network modeling.

Funding:To be updated
Affiliation:Washington State University
Status:Completed
Keywords:Graph machine learning, embeddings, statistical networks
